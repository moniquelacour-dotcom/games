Fluency Trail at Home

Grade 2 reading practice at home. Your child reads a short story aloud for one minute while you follow along and tap any word they miss. You'll see their speed, their accuracy, which kinds of words trip them up, and they earn stars as they go.

Everything you save stays on this computer, in this browser. Nobody else can see it, including your child's coach.

What it takes to pass

Scoring rules · tap to open

The Grade 2 fluency check

  • 104 words correct per minute reading a Grade 2 passage aloud
  • 90% accuracy or better
  • Both have to be met. Fast with too many misses doesn't pass.

How a read works

  • Sit side by side. Your child reads the top copy (colored rows). You tap misses on your scoring copy under it.
  • Pick a strategy as the focus first if you like. The cards above explain each one.
  • One read takes about 2 minutes with tagging. Reread the same passage 2–3 times and end on the best one.

How to score a read

  • Counts as a miss: a word read wrong, a word swapped for another, a skipped word, or a stall of about 3 seconds (say the word and move on)
  • Doesn't count: fixing it themselves, repeating a word, adding a word, or an accent
  • Skipping a whole line: every word in it is a miss
  • Words correct per minute = words read − misses

These practice stories were written for this page. They are not the test's passages. The scoring rules are the standard ones for one-minute reading checks.

Strategies

Teach one, then pick it as the focus for the next read.

Expression

Read it the way you would say it out loud. Make it sound like talking, not like a robot.

Say

"Read that sentence again like you're telling it to a friend."

Try

Read one sentence in a robot voice, then in a storyteller voice. Which one sounds like the story?

Punctuation

Period: stop and take a breath. Comma: a short pause. Question mark: your voice goes up. Exclamation mark: say it with feeling. Quotation marks: a character is talking.

Say

"What does that mark tell your voice to do?"

Try

Pick Punctuation as the focus and the marks in the passage light up.

Accuracy

Look at every letter, all the way to the end of the word. Sound out the tricky part. Break a long word into chunks.

Say

"Look at the end of that word again. What sound does it make?"

Try

Go slow enough to be right. Speed comes after accuracy, and the test needs 90%.

Chunk it

Big word? Cover part of it with your finger. Read one chunk at a time, then put the chunks together.

Say

"Find the first chunk. Now the next one. Put them together."

Try

pan·cake, sun·flow·er, bas·ket. Every chunk has one vowel sound.

Check the ending

Look all the way to the end of the word. Endings like -s, -ed and -ing change the word.

Say

"Look at the end. What does it say?"

Try

jump, jumps, jumped, jumping. Read the base word first, then add the ending.

Read the middle

Don't stop at the first letter. The vowel in the middle tells you the sound.

Say

"What's the vowel in the middle? Short or long?"

Try

hat, hit, hot, hut. Then cap and cape: the silent e makes the vowel say its name.

Sight words

Some words don't follow the rules all the way. Sound out the regular part, and learn the tricky part by heart.

Say

"Which part is tricky? Let's put a heart on it."

Try

said, was, they, one, could, does. Flash a few before the read, then spot them in the story.

Reread

If it didn't make sense, or you lost your place, go back to the start of the sentence and read it again.

Say

"Let's go back to the start of that sentence."

Try

Good for practice reads. On the timed test, a reread costs time, so use it for the sentence that matters.

Does it sound right?

After you read a sentence, ask: does that sound like the way people talk? If not, look at the word again.

Say

"Does that sound right? Check the letters in that word."

Try

It's a check after sounding out, not a reason to guess. The fix is always to look back at the letters.

Does it make sense?

Make a picture in your head. If the picture doesn't work, find the word that doesn't fit and sound it out again.

Say

"Tell me what just happened in the story."

Try

After the minute, ask one question about the passage before showing the score.
