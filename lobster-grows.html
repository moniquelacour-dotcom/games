How a Lobster Grows

1Off the rocky coast of Maine, the cold ocean is full of lobsters. A lobster can live for many years and grow to be very large. But growing is not easy for a lobster. Its body is covered by a hard shell, and a hard shell cannot stretch.

A Shell on the Outside

2People have bones on the inside of their bodies. A lobster does not. Instead, it has an exoskeleton, a hard covering on the outside of its body. The exoskeleton protects the lobster's soft body from fish, crabs, and other hungry animals. It works like a suit of armor.

Time to Molt

3When a lobster gets too big for its shell, it must molt. To molt means to crawl out of an old shell. First, the lobster grows a new, soft shell under the old one. Next, the old shell cracks open along the back. Then the lobster slowly pulls itself out. This can take as long as half an hour. Finally, the lobster takes in lots of water, and its body swells to a bigger size before the new shell hardens.

A Risky Few Weeks

4Right after molting, a lobster is in danger. Its new shell is soft, so the lobster cannot protect itself well. Because of this, the lobster hides under rocks until its shell gets hard again. A new shell can take several weeks to harden. Some lobsters even eat their old shell! The old shell has minerals that help the new shell grow strong.

Lobster Facts

  • A young lobster may molt more than 20 times in its first five years.
  • A grown lobster molts about once a year, or even less.
  • A lobster can grow a new claw if it loses one.
